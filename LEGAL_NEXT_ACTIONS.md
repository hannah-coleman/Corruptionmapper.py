# Lawful next-action ladder

When a lead appears, work from the least intrusive action that can resolve it. A lead is not permission to investigate a person by any method available.

## Level 1: verify public material

- Read the complete official record, including attachments, corrections, and disposition.
- Check the official site, public docket, business registry, meeting archive, audit library, and public statements.
- Compare the source's date, version, issuer, and exact wording.
- Search for an independent primary source rather than more copies of the same report.

## Level 2: document the question

- State the neutral issue in one sentence.
- Identify the governing rule, policy, contract term, deadline, or professional standard.
- Record what the evidence proves, what it does not prove, and plausible innocent explanations.
- Add supporting, contradictory, and exculpatory material.

## Level 3: request public records

- Send a narrow request to the likely Arkansas or local custodian.
- Use federal FOIA only for records held by a federal agency.
- Request segregable non-exempt portions and electronic format when appropriate.
- Track submission, fees, clarification, production, redactions, denial reason, and appeal options.

## Level 4: counsel or qualified review

- Ask counsel about preservation letters, subpoenas, discovery, sealed records, privilege, retaliation, privacy, or publication.
- Ask a qualified expert about medical, pathology, biology, physics, environmental, or digital-forensic claims.
- Use an authorized safety or safeguarding channel for immediate threats or child-safety concerns.

## Prohibited shortcuts

Do not hack, probe, exploit, bypass authentication, defeat CAPTCHA, guess passwords, use stolen credentials, impersonate anyone, enter private groups deceptively, buy breached data, scrape around a technical block, install spyware, stalk, threaten, or bait a person into creating evidence.

Do not publish a person's private address, medical information, child information, disability information, credentials, or protected records. Do not automatically file complaints, contact subjects, or escalate flags. The mapper prepares an accountable handoff; a human and, where appropriate, counsel decide what happens next.
