# Reporting and escalation routing

This guide is a decision aid, not legal advice. Verify the current official contact page, jurisdiction, filing method, and deadlines before submitting. Ask counsel to review anything confidential, retaliatory, legally disputed, or potentially discoverable.

## Immediate danger

- Call local emergency services when there is an immediate threat to life or safety.
- Do not investigate, confront, bait, or publicly identify a suspected person during an active safety situation.
- Preserve only what is safe to preserve and tell counsel or a qualified advocate.

## Child safety

- Use the current official Arkansas child-protection reporting channel for suspected abuse or neglect when reporting is required or appropriate.
- For immediate danger, use emergency services first.
- Do not upload child-identifying details to the ordinary mapper, social media, or an informal tip channel.
- Ask counsel how to preserve, request, or disclose records; do not forward leaked child-welfare material.

## Medical, disability, and health records

- Route suspected provider, facility, licensing, or patient-safety issues to the appropriate state licensing or health oversight authority after counsel review.
- Route protected-record privacy concerns to the provider's privacy officer, the applicable federal or state privacy regulator, or counsel as appropriate.
- Do not send full medical records when a redacted summary or narrow description will establish the issue.
- Do not diagnose, infer causation, or publish health information from timing or symptoms alone.

## Law enforcement and corrections

- Start with the agency's official complaint, professional-standards, records, or internal-affairs process where appropriate.
- Consider the county prosecutor, state oversight, licensing/accreditation body, inspector, or civil-rights channel based on jurisdiction and counsel's advice.
- Preserve the agency's response, case number, deadlines, and appeal path.
- If the agency is itself the subject, do not assume it is the only reporting route.

## Courts and attorneys

- Procedural or filing issues go through the clerk or the court's established process.
- Attorney conduct goes to the appropriate bar or disciplinary authority after counsel review.
- Sealed records, discovery, subpoenas, preservation letters, and judicial remedies belong with your lawyer.
- A pleading is not a finding; read the order, judgment, disposition, and appeal status.

## Government, procurement, and ethics

- Send city or county process concerns to the appropriate clerk, records custodian, auditor, ethics body, or governing oversight channel.
- Send state-agency concerns to that agency's inspector, ombuds, ethics, audit, or complaint channel.
- Send federal-agency concerns to the agency inspector general, civil-rights office, or FOIA office as appropriate.
- Use the Arkansas public-records process for Arkansas and local records; use federal FOIA for federal agency records.
- Do not file the same unsupported allegation everywhere. Choose the office with authority over the specific record or conduct.

## Handoff packet

Before routing, prepare a neutral packet containing:

- one-sentence issue statement
- jurisdiction and why the recipient has authority
- chronology with dates and time zone
- exact source passages, URLs, capture dates, and hashes where available
- governing rule or policy
- established facts, allegations, inferences, unknowns, and exculpatory material
- discrepancy description and alternative explanations
- requested action, urgency, privacy concerns, and response deadline
- counsel review status and a safe contact method

## Mapper routing status

Use: `needs-review`, `counsel-review`, `ready-for-custodian`, `ready-for-oversight`, `submitted`, `acknowledged`, `responded`, `appeal-considered`, `closed-supported`, `closed-insufficient-evidence`, or `closed-out-of-jurisdiction`.

The mapper should recommend a destination but never submit a complaint, publish an accusation, or contact a subject automatically.
