# Corruption Mapper audit coverage matrix

This is a completeness checklist for a lawful, evidence-first public-interest audit. A checked category means it was reviewed and logged, not that wrongdoing was found.

## 1. Case definition and safety

- [ ] Define the protected interest and the concrete audit question.
- [ ] Record jurisdiction: city, county, state, federal, tribal, or private actor.
- [ ] Set start/end dates and explain any changes to scope.
- [ ] Identify immediate safety concerns and use counsel or emergency services for threats.
- [ ] Keep investigation activity separate from direct confrontation or public accusations.
- [ ] Record who may access the casefile and maintain an access log.

## 2. Governing rules and process

- [ ] Identify the controlling statute, regulation, ordinance, policy, contract clause, court order, or professional standard.
- [ ] Capture the version effective on the event date.
- [ ] Map required steps, decision-makers, deadlines, notices, approvals, appeal rights, and retention duties.
- [ ] Distinguish a mandatory requirement from guidance, custom, or best practice.
- [ ] Log exceptions, emergency procedures, delegated authority, and documented reasons.

## 3. Institutions and hierarchy

- [ ] City of Paragould departments, boards, commissions, clerk, finance, procurement, and elected officials.
- [ ] Greene County departments, Quorum Court, clerk, assessor, treasurer, sheriff, prosecutor, and oversight bodies.
- [ ] Arkansas state agencies, courts, Secretary of State, ethics or inspector oversight, and relevant licensing bodies.
- [ ] Federal agencies only where they actually hold responsive records.
- [ ] Contractors, vendors, nonprofits, professional firms, subsidiaries, registered agents, and successor entities.
- [ ] Record role and authority separately from personal association.

## 4. Records and data sources

- [ ] Contracts, solicitations, bids, scoring, award notices, amendments, invoices, purchase orders, payments, and audits.
- [ ] Agendas, minutes, attachments, recordings, attendance, notices, calendars, and executive-session records where lawfully releasable.
- [ ] Budgets, grants, payroll or public compensation, travel, reimbursements, donations, and lobbying disclosures.
- [ ] Court dockets, pleadings, orders, judgments, appeals, and publicly filed exhibits.
- [ ] Business formation filings, amendments, registered-agent history, annual reports, assumed names, liens, and licenses.
- [ ] Property and tax records where public and relevant.
- [ ] Police, jail, dispatch, incident, complaint, discipline, policy, use-of-force, and body-camera records where releasable.
- [ ] Public statements, press releases, official social posts, public meeting videos, and local reporting.
- [ ] Audit, compliance, accreditation, inspector, ethics, and legislative oversight reports.

## 5. Source integrity

- [ ] Original URL, issuing body or account, title, record type, publication date, and UTC capture time.
- [ ] Exact quotation, page/paragraph, file version, or video timestamp.
- [ ] Local copy and SHA-256 hash where lawful and technically possible.
- [ ] Collection method, access limits, robots/terms check, and chain of custody.
- [ ] Primary, secondary, derivative, anonymous, or user-generated source classification.
- [ ] Independence check: repeated copies of one report do not count as separate corroboration.

## 6. Entities, relationships, and money

- [ ] Stable identity resolution with disambiguation notes; do not merge people with similar names.
- [ ] Role, employer, authority, dates active, and source for each node.
- [ ] Direct, inferred, or unresolved relationship type and the source for every edge.
- [ ] Ownership, officers, registered agents, addresses, contracts, payments, grants, and shared counsel where public and relevant.
- [ ] Transaction date, amount, payer, payee, purpose, approval, and supporting document.
- [ ] Conflicts of interest, recusals, disclosures, vendor rotation, bid patterns, and related-party indicators as review questions.
- [ ] Avoid treating a shared address, meeting, friendship, or representation as proof of misconduct.

## 7. Timeline and discrepancy analysis

- [ ] Build an event timeline in local time and UTC where relevant.
- [ ] Compare creation, publication, approval, amendment, payment, complaint, response, and retention dates.
- [ ] Flag missing attachments, changed versions, inconsistent names, amounts, dates, votes, and procedural steps.
- [ ] Classify discrepancy: clerical, definitional, temporal, substantive, or unresolved.
- [ ] Preserve the exact passages side by side.
- [ ] Record innocent explanations and exculpatory evidence before elevating a pattern.
- [ ] Track what each institution knew, when it knew it, and what it did next without assuming intent.

## 8. Retaliation and threat documentation

- [ ] Record exact conduct, words, date, location, witnesses, and supporting source.
- [ ] Separate protected activity, adverse action, causation indicators, and legitimate alternative explanations.
- [ ] Preserve notices, calls, messages, filings, employment or service changes, and response chronology lawfully.
- [ ] Minimize sensitive medical, disability, child, home-address, and identifying information.
- [ ] Have counsel assess privilege, confidentiality, preservation, and litigation-hold issues.
- [ ] Never bait, impersonate, threaten, surveil, or publish identifying details to force a response.

## 9. Scientific and technical claims

- [ ] State the measurable observation before naming a mechanism.
- [ ] Identify baseline, control, validated method, chain of custody, and qualified expert.
- [ ] Record limitations, uncertainty, alternative explanations, and what would falsify the hypothesis.
- [ ] For biology/pathology: consent, clinical context, accredited testing, specimen integrity, and medical privacy.
- [ ] For physics/environmental claims: instrument calibration, logs, units, conditions, timing, and reproducibility.
- [ ] Keep scientific review separate from legal conclusions.

## 10. Requests, responses, and appeals

- [ ] Send each Arkansas public-records request to the likely custodian with a narrow description.
- [ ] Use federal FOIA only for federal agency records.
- [ ] Request segregable non-exempt portions and preferred electronic format.
- [ ] Track submission, acknowledgment, fees, clarification requests, production, redactions, denial reason, and deadlines.
- [ ] Preserve the original response and metadata.
- [ ] Route disputes, appeals, subpoenas, preservation letters, and privilege questions through counsel.

## 11. Logos review gates

Before a material claim enters a report:

- [ ] Observation is preserved exactly.
- [ ] Source is identified and accessible.
- [ ] Applicable rule or mechanism is cited.
- [ ] Claim is no broader than the evidence.
- [ ] Supporting and exculpatory sources are both logged.
- [ ] Independent corroboration was sought.
- [ ] Plausible and rejected explanations are recorded.
- [ ] Confidence and limitations are explicit.
- [ ] Privacy, safety, privilege, defamation, and publication risks were reviewed.
- [ ] Attorney or qualified expert review is complete where needed.

## 12. Status vocabulary

Use controlled statuses: `not-started`, `in-progress`, `awaiting-records`, `needs-corroboration`, `contradicted`, `supported`, `closed-no-issue`, `closed-insufficient-evidence`, and `counsel-review`.

Never use `guilty`, `corrupt`, or equivalent as an evidence status. Those are legal conclusions outside the mapper's authority.
